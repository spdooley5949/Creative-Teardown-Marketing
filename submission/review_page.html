What twelve fitness apparel brands are actually saying in Google search

A messaging teardown of competitor search ads, built to find the claim nobody is making. Read it, then tell me where I am wrong.

The finding

The category has run out of ways to say “comfortable.”

Every brand in the set is selling some blend of performance, comfort and versatility, and almost none are backing it with hard evidence. The category's discipline gap is proof, not positioning — with community and sustainability the least-defended themes.

59%of ads claim technical performance, across all 12 brands
6%claim community or belonging — 6 ads, 5 of them Gymshark’s
67%of ads carry no hard evidence: no rating, count, price or guarantee
16%of search ads are brand-level; the rest is product feed and discounts

Corroboration — a second, independent dataset

A teammate contributed 1,786 ads across eight brands, dated 2021 to 2026, gathered a completely different way: a year-stratified harvest with text pulled by OCR rather than transcribed by hand. Four brands appear in both datasets. Neither collector saw the other’s work, which makes the overlap a genuine replication test.

Different collector, different method, different brands, different years. Same answer. The gap is not an artifact of who gathered the ads or when.

What this does not support: the second dataset is year-stratified, so its brand mix swings hard between years — 2021 is 86% Adidas and Puma. Any year-over-year trend drawn from it would be measuring sample composition, not category behaviour. No trend is claimed here.

Method — what we are actually reading

Only a sixth of these ads are about the brand

Search results are dominated by product-feed and discount ads, which say nothing about how a brand positions itself. Every ad here is classified so the analysis can be run on brand-level copy alone. This is the honest boundary of what the finding covers.

Recommendation

Where to play

  1. 01
    Replace generic comfort and performance adjectives with one quantified proof per hero product, and lead the ad with it.
    Every brand claims technical performance and 67% of ads attach no hard evidence at all. Where proof exists it is almost entirely star ratings and offer terms. A number — grams saved, energy return, wash cycles held — is instantly differentiating in an auction where every competing headline sounds the same.
  2. 02
    Claim community and belonging as owned territory, with a named program and published participation data.
    Six ads across two brands, five of them Gymshark's coached programmes. The territory is not empty, it is uncontested: ten brands have ceded belonging to one competitor, and even Gymshark publishes no participation numbers. A measurable programme beats an unproven claim.
  3. 03
    Stop bidding into the versatility and comfort keywords where lululemon, Vuori and Fabletics are stacked.
    Everyday athleisure and versatility are the most saturated intersections in the data, which means the highest bid pressure. Size-inclusive (one ad, Fabletics) and yoga or studio (two ads) are materially cheaper ground.
  4. 04
    Audit low-volume review proof before scaling it.
    Tracksmith advertises on 22 reviews and Arc'teryx's store rating is 4.0 from 24, while Vuori runs 13,593 and Alo 21,615. If ratings are the category's default proof, volume and score are competitive assets, not hygiene.

Method — read this before quoting any number

What this analysis cannot tell you

Two datasets
The 101-ad primary set was transcribed by hand. The 1,786-ad replication set was harvested with tooling and OCR by a teammate. Different provenance, so the second is used as corroboration rather than primary evidence. Do not describe the project as entirely hand-collected.
No trend claims
The replication set carries real dates but is year-stratified, so its brand mix changes sharply year to year. Nothing here claims a year-over-year trend.
Source
Google Ads Transparency Center only. Public, no login. No Meta, TikTok, LinkedIn or organic.
“Listed,” not “live”
Filters applied were US region, text format and advertiser domain. The date filter was left on “Any time” and individual ads were not opened to check run dates. This is every text ad the Transparency Center listed for these advertisers on 12 August, not a verified set of ads running that moment. It does not change any finding, but “live” would overstate it.
Format
Text ads only. Most spend in this category is video and image creative, which carries no copy to analyze. This is a search messaging teardown, not a brand strategy audit.
Sample
101 ads. Depth is uneven by design: lululemon 18, Arc'teryx and On 12, down to 3 for Patagonia, which genuinely runs few search text ads. Cells are percentages so the comparison holds, but a 3-ad brand still carries a wide error bar.
Dates
The first-seen column is the collection date, not the true first-seen date. Do not cite it.
Tagging
Claude tagged every ad, then the tags were frozen to a committed file so the numbers reproduce exactly between runs. No human has reviewed the tags yet. That is the ask below.
Revision
An earlier version of this page, built on 42 ads, claimed Vuori, Gymshark and lululemon cited no proof. That was a sampling artifact and is corrected here. All twelve brands cite proof somewhere.
